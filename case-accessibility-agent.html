Accessibility Agent

I turned an existing internal Slack RAG bot into a reliable accessibility advisor for Redwood by rewriting its system prompt, designing its conversation behavior, and restructuring the source content it retrieves from.

Role
Prompt design, conversation design, content architecture
Surface
Slack bot backed by an internal knowledge base (RAG)
Users
Designers, PMs, and developers working in Redwood

The problem

Redwood has a large body of accessibility rules, and designers, PMs and developers kept pinging the accessibility team for answers. An internal Slack bot connected a knowledge base to an LLM, but retrieval pulled the wrong sections, tone drifted, and it answered things it should not. People got burned once and went back to asking humans.

The solution

I owned everything between retrieval and the reader: the prompt, the conversation behaviors and the shape of the content it retrieves.

01

A custom system prompt

Defines scope, how to cite component specs, and how confident the bot may sound when retrieval is thin.

02

Oracle voice in the model

The voice and tone guideline translated into explicit instructions, so every answer sounds like one product.

03

Behaviors for hard cases

A workflow for ambiguous, off-topic and out-of-scope questions: ask one clarifying question, decline briefly, or route to a human.

04

Structured source content

Reusable templates so every component’s accessibility spec has the same sections, making answers findable.

What I learned

Prompt quality has a ceiling set by content quality. Half of prompt engineering here was information architecture.
