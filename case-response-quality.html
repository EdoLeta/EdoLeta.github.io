Guideline: building high-quality AI responses

I wrote the guideline that defines what a good AI response looks like at Oracle, then converted it into prompt instructions so teams could enforce it instead of just reading it.

Role
Author and owner
Type
Standards document plus reusable prompt instructions
Reach
Internal teams, plus customers building on Oracle’s AI platforms

The problem

Dozens of teams shipped LLM features at once, each writing its own prompts and each landing on a different idea of “good.” One assistant hedged everything, another was curt, a third answered in bulleted walls. Customers saw one product with an inconsistent personality, and teams debugged tone and structure one prompt at a time.

The solution

I proposed, wrote and owned the guideline, with input from design, content, engineering and the teams closest to customer feedback. My focus was standardization: one shared definition of a good response for every team.

01

The guideline

A published standard for what a good response is: grounded, relevant, well structured, on voice, safe, and coherent across turns.

02

Prompt engineering guidance

Before-and-after examples of how prompt patterns change agent output, for the PMs and designers who write prompts.

03

Reusable prompt blocks

Each section of the guideline as a block to paste into a system prompt. Adopt the whole standard or only the pieces.

Example

A password reset question

One fictional user request, an original response, the rule I wrote, and the improved response with the changes marked.

Before: the original response

“Great question! Password resets can sometimes be tricky, and it really depends on a few things. In general you may be able to reset it, though this might vary depending on your setup. You could try looking for a link on the sign-in page that might say something like Forgot password, and if that does not work it is possible you will need to contact support.”

Hedges on facts it was given, buries the action, and opens with filler.

My decision: the guideline rule

Lead with the answer, and do not hedge on facts the assistant was given. In the prompt-instruction block it reads:

Lead with the direct answer in the first sentence.
State what the user can do before explaining why.
Do not hedge on facts you were given.
Add steps only when the user needs them.

Paraphrased for this example.

After: the improved response

“Select Forgot password on the sign-in page and follow the link we email you. 1 If it has not arrived in a few minutes, check your spam folder, then contact your administrator. 2”

1 The action comes first, with no filler or hedging. 2 The fallback is a short, ordered step instead of a list of maybes.

What I learned

A guideline is a product. It needs a user, a job to be done, and a form that fits the moment of use. Writing the document was a third of the work. Making it easy to copy and paste was the rest.
