Metrics and evaluations for conversational quality

I partnered with internal quality and engineering teams to define how Oracle measures single-turn and multi-turn AI conversations, on readability, completeness and word count, and how teams write test cases and run evaluations.

The decision

Score a reply against the whole conversation, not only the latest message.

Why. A reply can be right on its own and wrong in context. Scoring across turns catches that.

The problem

The response quality guideline said what good looks like, not how to prove it. Teams judged agents by reading a few transcripts, and two reviewers could disagree on the same one. Multi-turn made it worse: a reply can be right on its own and wrong in context.

What we measure

We looked at both single-turn and multi-turn conversations. Across both, three measures matter most.

01

Readability

How easily a person can read and scan the response.

02

Completeness

Whether the response covers everything the user asked.

03

Word count

Whether the length fits the question: long enough to help, short enough to read.

The solution

I owned the metric definitions, the anchor examples and the test-case guidance. Partner teams brought the evaluation infrastructure. My focus was standardization: one way to measure quality that every team uses.

01

Single-turn scoring

Each response is scored on its own, with a definition, a scale and anchor examples.

ReadabilityCompletenessWord count
02

Multi-turn scoring

The same measures applied across a whole conversation, so a reply that is fine alone can still be caught when it fails in context.

ReadabilityCompletenessWord count
03

Test-case guide

How to write a case someone else can run: goal, context, observable criteria and failure modes, with a template.

04

Evaluation procedure

Human versus model grading, rater calibration, sample sizes, and reporting that compares across releases.

What I learned

Defining a metric is a conversation design problem. The hard part is not the scale, it is writing anchor examples so two reasonable people score the same transcript the same way.
